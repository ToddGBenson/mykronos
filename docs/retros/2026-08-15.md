# Retro — 2026-08-15: the day the platform was its own first incident

One operational day: a production outage, a credential expiry, a resource-starvation
cascade, the gate refusing its own author's commit, and the dashboard learning to tell
the truth about coverage. Eleven commits, three pipelines re-applied, two prod deploys
of TheHub (its first ever through the gated chain), and one lesson promoted
([L0003](../lessons/0003-a-check-that-cannot-report-is-a-check-that-does-not-exist.md)).

## What went well

- **The platform caught its own defects, in production, as designed.** The severity-floor
  gate (D-048) refused a commit that introduced a real finding — mine. The stages
  cross-check found the unit lane's lifetime of silence within an hour of existing.
  Absence-reconciliation, dispositions, and the audit trail all did their jobs under
  real load. This is the demo working, witnessed rather than claimed.
- **Every fix landed with the test that would have caught it**, and the drift-guard
  pattern (a test asserting a whole class cannot recur) was used four times.
- **Root causes over restarts.** Six distinct faults traced to mechanism before any
  state was touched; the one near-catastrophe — deploying prod TheHub under a compose
  project that didn't own the data volume — was caught by reading before acting.
- **The deploy poller's new log paid for itself within one cycle**, converting two
  invisible failures into two named ones.

## What went badly

- **A 24-hour warning went unread by the person who caused it (me).** Granting `qa`/`ai`
  superseded the token; the CLI said so; the significance was missed and the pipelines
  carried a dying credential to the minute of its expiry. The fix (deadline in the
  header, error-level escalation) treats the symptom properly, but the miss was human.
- **Deploy lag was asked about twice before it was addressed once.** Prod trailed main
  by hours repeatedly, and the operator had to ask "why don't I see my changes?" —
  the exact question a *security platform's own dashboard* should answer. Still open
  as the "prod is N commits behind" indicator.
- **Premature deploys, three times**: `deploy.ps1` pulls `:latest`, promote moves
  `:latest`, and I deployed before the right promote more than once. The gate held —
  nothing wrong shipped — but each was a wasted cycle that a version-visible deploy
  script would have prevented.
- **Two sessions, one Concourse, no coordination channel.** A token rotation from one
  session expired the other's pipeline vars; a re-apply resurrected a paused DAST job
  twice. The script-enforced pause fixes the second; the first still relies on luck.

## Themes

1. **Silent success is the failure mode of this whole domain.** Every serious defect
   today was something green that should have been red (L0003). The countermeasure that
   worked every time: render the disagreement between two sources of truth.
2. **State an operator sets by hand does not survive automation.** The DAST pause, the
   token in `.env`, the `v1` tag — all were correct once and wrong later. What lasted:
   state re-asserted by scripts on every apply.
3. **One host is one blast radius.** ZAP starved production, CI, DNS, and Docker itself.
   D-053's real fix is a resource budget, not a pause.
